## How to Read Your Company’s Financial Reports


> **In this Topic, you will learn:**

- How the “Magic Three” reports are organized
- How the reports support and inform each other
- The key pieces of information that each contain
- Summary

## How the magic three are organized – The Balance Sheet

  - Formula:
    - Assets – Liabilities = Equities
  - Section Organization:
    - Assets and Liabilities are organized in order of liquidity
    - Equities is organized by components of Equity (stock, retained earnings, etc.)
      - Represents what the owners get after all Liabilities have been satisfied

## How the magic three are organized – The Profit and Loss Statement

- Three Main Sections: Revenue, Expenses, and Net Profit (Loss)
- Revenue Formula:
  - Gross Revenue – Cost of Goods Sold = Net Revenue
- Expenses
  - Can be alphabetized or organized by order of importance or grouped by customized categories with sub-totals, specific to your company or your industry’s standard
- Profit or Loss Formula:
  - Total Net Revenue – Total Expenses = Net Profit/Loss

## How the magic three are organized – The Cash Flows Statement PT. 1 Description

- Three Sections: Operating Activities, Investing Activities, Financing Activities
- OPERATING ACTIVITIES:
  - Formula
    - Net Profit (Loss) +/- Net Decrease (Increase) in Receivables +/- Net Increase (Decrease) in Payables = Cash Provided by (Used by) Operations
  - How much of your Net Profit or Loss is derived from Cash activity vs. customer receivables and vendor payables
- INVESTING ACTIVITIES:
  - Formula
    - + Proceeds from sale of Assets – Cost of purchasing new Assets – Cost of Improving or Adding to Existing Assets = Cash Provided by (Used by) Investing Activities
  - How have you invested Cash not used for operations – inventory, equipment, real estate, other capital expenditures;
  - How have you converted Assets into Cash to use for operation or set aside for reserve
- FINANCING ACTIVITIES:
  - Formula
    - + Cash from founders and investors + Cash from loans and lines of credit – paydown of loan and line of credit principal – distribution to founders and investors = Cash Provided by (Used by) Financing Activities
  - How much was received from or distributed to founders and investors;
  - Are you reinvesting or distributing Cash profit

## How the magic three are organized – The Cash Flows Statement PT. 2 Operating Activities

- Three Sections: Operating Activities, Investing Activities, Financing Activities
- OPERATING ACTIVITIES:
  - Formula
    - Net Profit (Loss)
    - +/- Net Decrease (Increase) in Receivables
    - +/- Net Increase (Decrease) in Payables
    - = Cash Provided by (Used by) Operations
  - How much of your Net Profit or Loss is derived from Cash activity vs. customer receivables and vendor payables

## How the magic three are organized – The Cash Flows Statement PT. 3 Investing Activities

- Three Sections: Operating Activities, Investing Activities, Financing Activities
- INVESTING ACTIVITIES:
  - Formula
    - + Proceeds from sale of Assets
    - – Cost of purchasing new Assets
    - – Cost of Improving or Adding to Existing Assets
    - = Cash Provided by (Used by) Investing Activities
  - How have you invested Cash not used for operations – inventory, equipment, real estate, other capital expenditures;
  - How have you converted Assets into Cash to use for operation or set aside for reserve

## How the magic three are organized – The Cash Flows Statement PT. 4 Financing Activities

- Three Sections: Operating Activities, Investing Activities, Financing Activities
- FINANCING ACTIVITIES:
  - Formula
    - + Cash from founders and investors
    - + Cash from loans and lines of credit
    - – paydown of loan and line of credit principal
    - – distribution to founders and investors
    - = Cash Provided by (Used by) Financing Activities
  - How much was received from or distributed to founders and investors;
  - Are you reinvesting or distributing Cash profit

## How the reports support and inform each other

- The magic of the Balance Sheet, Profit and Loss Statement, and Cash Flows Statement is how they each inform the other and illuminate the true health of your company
- The Balance Sheet’s Assets section relies on both The Profit and Loss Statement and the Cash Flows Statement to calculate the remaining basis in or balance of Cash, Accounts Receivable, Allowance for Doubtful Accounts, Equipment and/or other Depreciable Assets used in operations to generate revenue
- The Balance Sheet’s Liabilities section relies on both the Profit and Loss Statement and the Cash Flows Statement to calculate the remaining basis in or balance of Accounts Payable, Accrued Expenses, Taxes and Payroll Liabilities, Loans Payable and other liabilities incurred in operations
- The Balance Sheet’s Equity section relies on the Profit and Loss Statement to calculate Retained Earnings (also called accumulated earnings or undistributed earnings); that is how much of the company’s net profit is reinvested or distributed
- The Balance Sheet’s Equity section relies on the Cash Flows Statement to calculate the cumulative amount founders and investors have contributed to the company, how much the company has distributed to them, and how much of their investment remains in the company

## What are the key pieces of information that each contains that you need to know

- Here are some key pieces of information contained in the magic three reports that you need to know:
- Balance Sheet
  - The company’s short-term liquidity or working capital (short-term liquid assets - short-term liabilities)
    - It is important for managing cash flow, determining financing needs, and performing financial analysis
  - The company’s financial leverage or debt-to-equity ratio (Total Liabilities / Total Equity)
    - It lets you know how your company is financing its operations – is it mostly through debt (total liability) or by the company founders and investors (total equity).
- Profit and Loss Statement
  - True cost of goods sold or cost of sale
  - True fixed cost vs. true variable cost of operations
  - What accounts for your company’s profit margin – increased revenue or decreased cost?
- Cash Flows Statement
  - What activity has the biggest impact on cash – operating, investing, or financing? Is it using or providing cash?
  - What is the major source of financing for company? Debt (short- or long-term), equity (founders and investors), or reinvested profits
