## How to Read Your Company’s Financial Reports

> **In this Topic, you will learn:**

- How the “Magic Three” reports are organized
- How the reports support and inform each other
- The key pieces of information that each contain

## The Balance Sheet

:::formula
Assets − Liabilities = Equities
:::

Section organization:

- **Assets and Liabilities** are organized in order of liquidity
- **Equities** is organized by components of Equity (stock, retained earnings, etc.) — it represents what the owners get after all Liabilities have been satisfied

## The Profit and Loss Statement

Three main sections: **Revenue**, **Expenses**, and **Net Profit (Loss)**.

:::formula
Gross Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold = Net Revenue
Total Net Revenue − Total Expenses = Net Profit (Loss)
:::

- **Expenses** can be alphabetized or organized by order of importance, or grouped by customized categories with sub-totals — specific to your company or your industry’s standard

## The Cash Flows Statement

Three sections, each answering a different question about your cash:

:::cards
- **Operating Activities** — how much of your Net Profit or Loss is derived from cash activity vs. customer receivables and vendor payables
- **Investing Activities** — how you invested cash not used for operations, and how you converted assets into cash
- **Financing Activities** — how much was received from or distributed to founders, investors, and lenders
:::

## Cash Flows — Operating Activities

:::formula
Net Profit (Loss)
+/− Net Decrease (Increase) in Receivables
+/− Net Increase (Decrease) in Payables
= Cash Provided by (Used by) Operations
:::

- Shows how much of your Net Profit or Loss is derived from cash activity vs. customer receivables and vendor payables

## Cash Flows — Investing Activities

:::formula
+ Proceeds from sale of Assets
− Cost of purchasing new Assets
− Cost of Improving or Adding to Existing Assets
= Cash Provided by (Used by) Investing Activities
:::

- How you have invested cash not used for operations — inventory, equipment, real estate, other capital expenditures
- How you have converted Assets into cash to use for operations or set aside for reserve

## Cash Flows — Financing Activities

:::formula
+ Cash from founders and investors
+ Cash from loans and lines of credit
− Paydown of loan and line of credit principal
− Distribution to founders and investors
= Cash Provided by (Used by) Financing Activities
:::

- How much was received from or distributed to founders and investors
- Whether you are reinvesting or distributing cash profit

## How the reports support and inform each other

The magic of the Balance Sheet, Profit and Loss Statement, and Cash Flows Statement is how they each inform the other and illuminate the true health of your company.

- The Balance Sheet’s **Assets** section relies on both the Profit and Loss Statement and the Cash Flows Statement to calculate the remaining basis in or balance of Cash, Accounts Receivable, Allowance for Doubtful Accounts, Equipment and/or other Depreciable Assets used in operations to generate revenue
- The Balance Sheet’s **Liabilities** section relies on both the Profit and Loss Statement and the Cash Flows Statement to calculate the remaining basis in or balance of Accounts Payable, Accrued Expenses, Taxes and Payroll Liabilities, Loans Payable and other liabilities incurred in operations
- The Balance Sheet’s **Equity** section relies on the Profit and Loss Statement to calculate Retained Earnings (also called accumulated earnings or undistributed earnings); that is how much of the company’s net profit is reinvested or distributed
- The Balance Sheet’s **Equity** section relies on the Cash Flows Statement to calculate the cumulative amount founders and investors have contributed to the company, how much the company has distributed to them, and how much of their investment remains in the company

## What are the key pieces of information that each contains

:::compare
### Balance Sheet
- The company’s **short-term liquidity or working capital** (short-term liquid assets − short-term liabilities) — important for managing cash flow, determining financing needs, and performing financial analysis
- The company’s **financial leverage or debt-to-equity ratio** (Total Liabilities ÷ Total Equity) — lets you know how your company is financing its operations: mostly through debt, or by the company founders and investors
### Profit & Loss Statement
- True **cost of goods sold** or cost of sale
- True **fixed cost vs. true variable cost** of operations
- What accounts for your company’s **profit margin** — increased revenue or decreased cost?
### Cash Flows Statement
- What activity has the **biggest impact on cash** — operating, investing, or financing? Is it using or providing cash?
- What is the **major source of financing** — debt (short- or long-term), equity (founders and investors), or reinvested profits?
:::

## Summary

> **In this Topic you learned:**

- The magic three reports work together to illuminate the true health of your company
- The Balance Sheet’s sections are informed by key information from the Profit and Loss Statement and the Cash Flows Statement
- The Balance Sheet is organized in three sections: Assets, Liabilities, and Equities and its formula is: Assets – Liabilities = Equities
- The Profit and Loss Statement organized in three sections: Revenue, Expenses, Net Profit (Loss) and two important formulae are:
  - Revenue formula: Gross Revenue – Cost of Goods Sold = Net Revenue
  - Net Profit (Loss) formula: Total Net Revenue – Total Expenses = Net Profit (Loss)
- The Cash Flows Statement is organized in three activities: Operating, Investing, and Financing and lets you know how cash was used or provided by each activity
