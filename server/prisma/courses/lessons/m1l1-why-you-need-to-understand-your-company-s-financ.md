## Why You Need to Understand Your Company’s Financial Information

> **In this Topic, you will learn:**

- Knowing for certain vs having an idea of what the current state of your company is
- Being confident about vs. being unsure about how effectively you’re using cash to operate and grow your company
- Knowing how much financing you’re qualified for vs. how much financing you think you need
- Is your scaling plan based on accurate information or inconsistent information
- How to use financials to make sure that you make payroll and other operational obligations

## Knowing for Certain vs. Having an Idea

As a founder and being involved in the day-to-day operations of your business, you have an idea of how your business is doing. However, nothing beats having evidence that lets you know exactly how your business is doing.

Knowing for certain means that you have evidence of:

:::cards
- **What’s working** in your business operations
- **What’s not working** in your business operations
- The added cost and/or **loss of revenue and profit** from what’s not working
- Whether or not you are **ready for the next round of financing** for your business
- Whether or not it is the right time to **upscale or downsize** your business
:::

> There are other things you can think of that will give you certainty about your business. Write them down — and check whether you have the evidence for each.

## Being confident about vs. being unsure about how effectively you’re using cash

Cash is king, as the saying goes — but for any business, cash is the life blood of the company. So, having evidence-based understanding of how effectively you are using your company’s cash is an absolute imperative.

Being confident means that you have evidence of:

:::cards
- How much cash is **generated from and used to fund operations**
- How much cash is used to make **capital expenditures**
- How much cash is **financed** by you, investors, and lenders
- How much cash is **reinvested** in operations or withdrawn from the company
- Having a sufficient **cash reserve** or needing to increase it
:::

> There are other reasons you may have for wanting to be confident about your cash flows. Write those reasons down.

## Knowing how much financing you’re qualified for vs. how much you think you need

All companies seek financing at various points in their existence, for lots of reasons. When seeking financing — whether from family, friends, financial institutions, angel investors, or venture capital — you must be able to explain your company’s current financial position and why you’re seeking more money.

Knowing how much financing you qualify for means that you have evidence of:

:::cards
- What **assets and their valuation** are available for securing debt and/or equity financing
- What your **working capital requirements** are, if you’re financing working capital
- How much financing you can **service**, if you are pursuing debt financing
- **How you will use the funds** — in operations, capital expenditure, market penetration, opportunity, etc.
- Current and proposed **equity structure and capitalization**, if seeking equity financing
:::

> There are lots of other reasons why you may seek financing for your company. Write a few down.

## Is your scaling plan based on accurate information or inconsistent information

It is great to know that your hard work and efforts have gotten you to this point. You are ready to scale or grow your operations — demand for your product or service is high, and customers are lining up.

Now the big challenge is creating a workable and practical scaling plan. Company financial reports are one of the best sources of information available to determine the money you’ll need to scale effectively.

Creating a scaling plan based on accurate information means that you have evidence of:

:::cards
- **Cost** of implementing and managing your scaling plan
- How much can be **financed from proceeds from operations** (net profit) and how much needs to be financed by other sources
- How the plan will **impact cash flows and cash reserves**
:::

> There are other pieces of information specific to your company — write them down.

## How to use financials to make sure that you make payroll and other operational obligations

For any business, having sufficient funds to cover recurring payroll and other operational costs can be challenging at times. Having a plan or budget to manage operational costs will help you get through those challenging times. However, a plan or budget is only as good as the source of the information used to create it.

Keeping financials accurate and up-to-date makes them a great source of evidence for:

:::cards
- **Predicting** short, mid-, and long-term cash requirements for payroll and other recurring operational costs
- **Projecting** short, mid-, and long-term revenues
- Determining when to **draw down** on lines of credit or other borrowing options
:::

> There is other key information specific to your business that you need to make sure you can make payroll and other operational obligations. Write it down.

## Summary

> **In this Topic you learned that:**

- Your company’s financial reports provide important evidence that supports all your decisions
- Your financial reports lets you know for certain how your business operations are doing; what’s working, what’s not working
- Cash is king and the life blood of your company
- Having up-to-date and accurate financials will help you be more proactive and effective in managing your company’s cash flows
- Having up-to-date and accurate financials provide evidence to support your estimates of cash needs and how the company can service the cost of capital (interest, dividend, closing cost)
- Having up-to-date and accurate financials is an important resource for creating and managing your company’s scaling or growth plan
