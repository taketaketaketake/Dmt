## How you use cash to run your company

- Cash is the life blood of your company
- How you use it to run your company could either help it grow or put it out of business
- Therefore, it is imperative that you know:
  - How you use cash to run your business
  - What accounts for the inflows and outflows of Cash
  - How much you need or use in operations
  - How much you spend for capital expenditure
  - How much you finance through debt and/or your own and investors funds

## How much cash is used in operations

- If cash is the life blood of any company, then operations is the heart of it
- To keep the heart of your company healthy, you must use Cash efficiently and effectively in operations
- The Cash Flows Statement helps you understand how efficiently and effectively Cash is both provided by and used in operations
- Net Profit (Loss) Before Depreciation & Amortization Expense
- Current Revenue Uncollected
- Past Revenue Collected
- Past Expenses Paid
- Current Expenses Unpaid
- Cash Used in Operations

## How much cash is used to make capital expenditures

- Capital expenditures (CapEx) are funds used by a company to acquire, upgrade, and maintain physical assets such as property, plants, buildings, technology, or equipment.*
- CapEx is often used to undertake new projects or investment by a company.*
- The Investments Activities section of the Statement of Cash Flows lets you know
  - How much the company spends on buying physical assets
  - How much proceeds were received from selling physical assets
- How much and how frequently you use Cash to make Capital Expenditure depends on
  - The age and remaining useful life of physical assets used in operation
  - Condition of those physical assets (new, used, in need of repair, or replacement)
  - Update or change in processing methodology, practices, and procedures requiring new physical assets
- And there are specific reasons why your company would use Cash to make capital expenditures.

## How much cash is used to paydown or payoff financing debt

- For any company, debt can be both good and bad
- Debt can be good because
  - It can be used to keep the company operations going
  - It can be used to make necessary improvements in machines and other physical assets
  - It can be used to retire more expensive debt
- Debt can be bad because
  - It could be too expensive to service (high interest rate or unfavorable payment terms)
  - It could be used to buy depreciating assets with decreasing value
- The Financing section of the Cash Flows Statement lets you know how much cash is
  - Borrowed from lenders
  - Used to pay down principal on debt

## Is there sufficient cash to cover the next month or 6 months’ operations

- Effective management of Cash is critical for the operation of all companies
- Because operations is typically ongoing, it is imperative to know that there is sufficient cash to cover the next month or several months’ operating costs and expenses
- The Statement of Cash Flows lets you know whether you have sufficient cash to continuing operating or need to seek financing

## Can you purchase new equipment or make other capital expenditure with or without financing

- The Statement of Cash Flows and Cash Flow Projection help you determine how to finance the purchase of new equipment and other capital expenditures
- The Statement of Cash Flows lets you know how much cash is available for operations, investing, and financing
- The Cash Flow Projection helps you to plan for such purchases by projecting out cash commitments coming due in the near term

## How much did you put into the company

- Whether you contributed cash, other assets, or sweat equity into your company, you should know its exact value
- The Financing Activities section of the Statement of Cash Flows lets you know how much Cash you:
  - contributed
  - withdrew
- This section also let’s you know how much Cash you
  - borrowed from debtors
  - paid back debtors
  - received from investors
  - paid back investors

## How soon is credit sales converted to cash

- For any company, the sooner that credit sales can be converted to cash, the better
- To know how long it takes for credit sales to convert to cash
  - From the Cash Flow Statements – Operating Activities
    - Increase in Accounts Receivable signals higher revenue from credit sales than cash
  - From Receivables Report
    - Number of days it takes for credit sales to convert to cash
- You can manage the conversion of credit sales to cash in a number of ways
  - Factor receivables
  - Offer incentive to customers such as “pay now and save”

## What do you do when you have too much cash

- Having excess cash in your company is both good and bad
- It’s good because it
  - signals that the company has strong performance and has potential to be even more successful
- It’s bad because
  - Investors will wonder why you’re not putting it to use
- Here are some things you might do with your excess cash
  - Determine what your cash reserve should be and set it aside
  - Pay down or pay off company debt
  - Invest in updating or overhauling operations to stay competitive
  - Invest in inventory at bargain since you have more room to negotiate
- Have you thought about what you would do if your company had too much cash?
