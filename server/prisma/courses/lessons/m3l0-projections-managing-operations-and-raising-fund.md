## What is a Projection and How Do You Use it to Manage Operations and Raise Funds

> **In this Topic, you will learn:**

- What is a projection
- Different types of projections and their uses
- How to create realistic and reliable projections
- When to change projections and how to communicate that change
- Using projections to plan for growing or shifting your operations
- Using projections to explain and support the need to raise capital or seek financing
- Using cash flow projection to effectively manage current fixed and variable expenses, maturing commitments, recurring obligations, capturing new opportunities, and pay dividends

## What is a projection

:::formula
A projection is a forecast of expected future revenue, expenses, and cash flows for a future period.
:::

## Different types of projections and their uses

There are four key projections that you will need for your business:

:::cards
- **Sales Projection** — used to predict sales monthly, quarterly, annually. It is also used to predict and plan how best to use resources to generate sales or meet future demands
- **12-Month Balance Sheet Projection** — used to predict the big picture of your company based on projected sales, expenses, and cash flow activities such as investing and financing activities
- **Expense Budget** — used to predict recurring and variable expenses monthly, quarterly, annually to ensure that you are able to meet your sales targets. It also helps to identify areas of over- or under-spending
- **Cash Flows Projection** — used to predict how much cash is available to pay expenses and costs of operations, invest in machine, equipment, and technology, and satisfy outstanding debt. It is also used to anticipate future need for cash infusion
:::

## How to create realistic and reliable projections

The more realistic and reliable a projection is, the better you are:

- able to identify **risks and opportunities**
- able to take action that will get you the **results that you need and want**

The best way to create such projections:

- Start with **historical data** from your company’s operations contained in the balance sheet, profit and loss statement, and statement of cash flows
- Predict future outcomes based on that historical data and **current external information** about customers, vendors, industry trends, etc.

## When to change projections and how to communicate that

:::cards
- **Review regularly** — projections should be reviewed, compared against actual performance, and updated at least once or twice per year
- **Update on disruption** — more frequent updates should be made when something major happens that disrupts operations, sales, resources, or a change in executive management
- **Communicate fast** — such changes should be communicated as soon as possible to all executives, managers, employees, and other stakeholders
:::

## Using projections to plan for growing or shifting your operations

- A Cash Flow Projection can be used to plan for growth or shifting your operations
- You can model different scenarios of how cash used in one activity area could affect resources in another activity to predict how best to grow your business
- Such as: manage current fixed and variable expenses, maturing commitments, recurring obligations, capturing new opportunities, and pay dividends

## Using projections to raise capital or seek financing

:::reveal What do investors want your Cash Flow Projection to tell them?
- **Why** you are raising capital
- **How** it will help the company scale or grow
- How they can realize a **healthy return** on their investment
:::
