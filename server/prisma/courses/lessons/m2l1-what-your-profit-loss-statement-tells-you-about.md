## Is the company profitable

The Profit and Loss Statement gives you a summary of revenue generated, costs and expenses incurred in your business operations, and lets you know whether your company earned profit or not.

There are two primary basis by which to calculate profit and loss:

:::compare
### Cash Basis
Considers revenue when it is earned and cash is received. Costs and expenses are considered when they are incurred and paid.
### Accrual Basis
Considers revenue when it is earned. Costs and expenses are considered when they are incurred.
:::

:::formula
Total Revenue − Total Costs and Expenses = Net Profit (Loss)
:::

- Your company is **profitable** when its total revenues are greater than total costs and expenses
- Your company is **not profitable** when its total revenues are less than total costs and expenses

## What is your breakeven revenue, i.e., how much revenue you need to cover expenses

Breakeven lets you know how much revenue you need to generate to cover your total expenses.

:::formula
Breakeven: Total Revenue = Total Expenses
Total Expenses = Fixed Cost + Variable Cost
:::

- Breakeven can be determined by the number of units or revenue
- Breakeven lets you know what you can spend to acquire a customer or increase sales to current customer

## What are your sources of revenue and how has it changed from last reporting period

- Sources of revenue vary from company to company
- Some companies’ revenue is derived from selling only one product or service, others from selling multiple
- Still other companies have other sources of revenue that are considered one-time or sourced outside of normal operations; these may be excluded when analyzing profitability from operations
- Whether your company sells one or more products or services, you will most likely have different prices per unit based on your company’s pricing method and customer demand
- To understand what drives revenue and net profit/loss, you must know which product(s) or service(s) generates the most revenue and profit as well as which ones do not
- Further, you will want to perform period to period analysis to identify any significant changes in sources of revenue

This will help you better project revenue, cash flows, financing needs, and attract strategic investors.

## Is there an opportunity to diversify your revenue stream

Once you have a good handle on your company’s sources of revenue, you are better able to identify opportunities to diversify revenue:

:::cards
- **Variations** — Are there variations of your product that can be a new revenue stream?
- **New purposes** — Are there new purposes for your product that can be a new revenue stream?
- **Packages & combinations** — Are there new pricing packages or product combinations that can be offered?
- **Know the breakeven** — For each new revenue stream, what is the breakeven revenue or unit?
:::

## Is your company dependent on one major customer or vendor and what does that mean

It is always a concern for any business when there is one major customer who generates the lion’s share of its total revenue or a major vendor who supplies the most important material or components of their product.

This is extremely important to know because if, for any reason, you lose this customer or vendor, your business is at risk.

:::reveal Are your biggest customers costing you more than they’re worth?
- When you are reviewing your profit and loss statement, you should know or find out who or what company(ies) account for the majority of your recurring revenue and, more importantly, profit.
- You should know what it costs to realize revenue from such customers and vendors because their impact on your bottom line may not be as positive as you believe.
- By knowing the cost to service a major customer vs. a small customer, you can determine which is more profitable and worth having as a customer — it is often more profitable to service small customers than to have one major customer.
:::

## Is revenue growing or decreasing

There are any number of reasons why your business’ revenue grows or decreases. Analyzing and comparing your profit and loss statement over several reporting periods can help you find those reasons and reduce or eliminate the risk it may cause.

Here are some possible reasons for the increase or decrease in revenue:

:::cards
- New sales during a strong sales cycle
- New large customer
- New types of customers
- Higher repeat sales
- Loss of repeat customer
- Off season cycle
- Little to no new customers
:::

In addition to knowing the reasons why, you want to know the frequency and rate at which your revenue is growing or decreasing so that you can gauge the severity of the risk to your business and how much time you have to take action to mitigate the risk.

## Is revenue growing faster than expenses or vice versa

- You not only need to understand how fast revenue is growing or declining, but you also need to know the relationship of revenue to expenses
- This gives you insight into how effective your company’s operations is in managing both growth and decline in revenue
- Process or procedure that is most or least effective
- Category of or specific expense that is most sensitive to changes in revenue, e.g., production, packaging, sales, payroll, etc.

## What are the company’s biggest expenses

There are two broad components of the Cost of Operations. To effectively manage the Cost of Operations, you must know the details of each:

:::compare
### Cost of Goods Sold
The direct costs of producing the product — e.g., cost of material, labor, and manufacturing overhead. Also called Cost of Sales.
### Operating Expenses
The indirect costs such as selling expenses, advertising, delivery, general and administrative, indirect labor, etc.
:::

Knowing the biggest expenses and effectively managing them means that you are:

- Better able to ensure that your company meets its Net Profit goals
- Better able to plan for any significant change that impacts Net Profit and margins
- More proactive about keeping those expenses in line with the company’s budget

## How much has the cost of operations increased or decreased from the last reporting period

For any business, the cost of operations fluctuates from one reporting period to the next. Analyzing your company’s Profit and Loss Statements over multiple reporting periods is an excellent way to both quantify and account for fluctuations.

Quantifying and accounting for the reasons for the fluctuation enable you to:

- Be more proactive and strategic in mitigating the frequency and severity of those fluctuations
- Reduce the impact on Net Profit and Profit Margin
