## What is a budget and should you have one

:::formula
A budget is a set of parameters and limits that you create to monitor and control your company’s spending.
:::

- A budget uses estimates of future revenue and expenses for a specified period of time
- It is reviewed and compared to actual performance on a periodic basis
- Budgets are an integral part of running any business efficiently and effectively *(Investopedia)*

## How it works

- The process begins by establishing **assumptions** — such as “sales will increase by 5% in 3 months” — for the upcoming budget period
- These assumptions are related to projected sales trends, cost trends, and the overall economic outlook of the market, industry, or sector
- Specific factors affecting potential expenses, such as planned hiring or variable marketing expenses, are addressed and monitored *(Investopedia)*

## What sources of information to use to create a budget

:::cards
- **Historical performance** drawn from company balance sheet, profit and loss statement, and cash flows statement
- **Market assumptions** by credible market analysts and your own observations
- Current **vendor relationships** and contracts
- Current **customer relationships** and contracts
- **Marketing plan** and sales pipeline
:::

## When and how to modify a budget

There are two kinds of budgets used in a company:

:::compare
### Static
Does not change for the entire duration of the budget.
### Flexible
Can be altered and adapted throughout the duration of the budget.
:::

- Budgets are typically modified and updated yearly — though a company may choose to modify and update their budget more frequently
- Modify budgets to **move money** from one expense to another
- Modify budgets to **capture a profitable opportunity**

## Establishing procedures and processes for creating and modifying a budget

- The standards and procedures used to develop and modify a budget include assumptions about the markets, key relationships with vendors and customers, and explanations of how certain calculations were made
- The **sales projection** is often the first to be developed, followed by expense budgets based on future cash inflows
- For larger corporations, specific budgets are developed for all the different subsidiaries, divisions, and departments within an organization
- For a manufacturer, a separate budget is often developed for direct materials, labor, and overhead
- All budgets get rolled up into the **master budget**, which also includes a budgeted balance sheet, a projection of cash inflows and outflows, and an overall financing plan *(Investopedia)*

## Comparing budget to actual spending

Comparing actual results against budget allows you to:

- Measure your **year-to-date performance**
- Judge whether the company is **trending ahead or behind plan**
- **Adjust management tactics** accordingly

:::formula
Without reports to measure your performance, you are flying blind in a snowstorm.
:::

## Using your budget to spot waste and fraud

Your budget is an excellent tool for spotting possible waste and fraud. When reviewing and comparing budget to actual, the following inconsistencies are possible red flags that may signal employee or vendor fraud:

:::cards
- **Unreconciled cash transactions** over 30 days old
- **Unidentified vendors** with repeated or one-time payments
- Insistence on keeping **paper receipts and records**
- Frequent **duplicate vendor payments**
- Regular **inventory shortfalls** at physical counts
- Increasing **vendor expenses as a % of sales**
- Increasing **payroll without hiring** new staff
- Continuously **shrinking cash** despite profitable financial reporting
:::

All of the above can appear as separate instances or all at once, and are each cause for inquiry and/or investigation.

## Leveraging budget to create projections

Once you have budgets in place, you refer to them when creating projections to ensure that:

- There is sufficient cash to fund any budget overages, and cash freed up from unused or underused budget lines is utilized
- You have **contingency plans** for budget shortfall or surplus
- Your **90-day sales pipeline** and marketing initiatives can be proactively managed
