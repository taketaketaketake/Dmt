## The Art of Financial Storytelling

For thousands of years, storytelling has been the most effective form of communicating ideas, experiences, beliefs, history, knowledge, and wisdom.

- When raising capital, the conversation starts with the **story of your business**
- It describes your journey from the first idea through the struggle and challenge, and final achievement of creating your business and proving its viability

:::formula
Your company’s financials are a key element of that story — helping to prove the truth and possibility of your vision and business.
:::

## Outline for Telling Your Company’s Story

:::cards
- **Background and conditions** — how and why you started; establish credibility
- **The problem and struggle** — explain the problem and your struggle or quest to solve it
- **The breakthrough** — find or create something that solves the problem
- **The result** — creating a business to deliver the solution
- **The promise** — to customers, the mission of the company; to investors, the vision of what you’re building and that they could be part of something great
:::

## How to frame your company’s financials with storytelling

Here are areas where financial information can be used to help support the story:

:::cards
- **The financial impact of the problem** on the people affected by it — cost of the problem, cost of failed solutions, size of the market
- **The money spent developing a workable solution** — research and development, prototyping, market testing, proof of concept; sales to date, number of customers, testimonials, sales pipeline, number of stores selling your product
- **Current financial condition and what’s possible with funds raised** — sales forecasting, uses of funds, operating budget, cash flow projection, financial ratios, current and projected magic three reports
- **THE ASK** — how much you need, structure of the offer (equity or debt), next step
:::

## What investors and banks want to know about your company

Investors and banks have different criteria for evaluating companies to fund:

:::compare
### Banks
Funding companies is **not an investment** — they are looking for a safe return on money loaned. They want to see:
- Stability and consistent growth over time; at least 3 years of steady state financials
- Additional benefits to the bank for helping the business grow — e.g., expanding banking business (new accounts, payroll processing, employee banking incentives)
- Ability to service the bank loan
- Good character of founders and/or co-signers
### Investors
It **is an investment** — they are looking for potential for a significant return. They want to see:
- Your vision and business are congruent with their expertise, experience, and investment criteria
- How much debt is on the books relative to equity
- Marketing plans and sales projections
- Ratios: financial, activity (key), solvency, liquidity, and profitability
- Current financials: balance sheet, profit and loss statements, and cash flow statements
- Current valuation
:::

## Tailoring your presentation to their funding criteria

Before making that first call, do your homework. Find out as much as possible about the company, bank, or investor to whom you are presenting:

- **Who they are** — name, location, CV, social media presence, LinkedIn profile
- **How they prefer to be solicited** — in person, phone interview, video presentation, long or short meeting, multiple meetings and follow up
- **What their funding criteria are** — typical stage of company, industry or markets, funding size, founder profile, geography, specialty, business model, what’s possible, what’s probable

Determine that you and the investor are a possible good fit — then customize your pitch deck to reflect how you and the investor are a good match, and that you meet their criteria for funding.

## How to stand in your company’s history, vision, and goals

The act of taking a stand in and of itself does not need evidence. However, providing evidence to back up your stand enables others to buy in to your resolve:

:::cards
- Your **history** demonstrates your passion, commitment, and leadership
- Your **vision** is what you are building and what is possible for the business and the world
- Your **goals** are clear and specific — bold yet achievable
:::

Having done all this, you are well on your way to a “Yes.”
