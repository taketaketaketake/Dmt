## The Art of Financial Storytelling

- For thousands of years, storytelling has been the most effective form of communicating ideas, experiences, beliefs, history, knowledge, and wisdom.
- When raising capital, the conversation starts with the story of your business.
- It describes your journey from the first idea through the struggle and challenge, and final achievement of creating your business and proving its viability.
- Your company’s financials are a key element of that story, helping to prove the truth and possibility of your vision and business

## Outline for Telling Your Company’s Story

- Background and conditions (how/why you started)
  - Establish credibility
- The problem and struggle
  - Explain the problem and your struggle or quest to solve it
- The breakthrough
  - Find or create something that solves the problem
- The result
  - Creating a business to deliver the solution
- The promise for customers and investors
  - Promise to customers is the mission of the company
  - Promise to investors is the vision of what you’re building and that they could be part of something great

## How to frame your company’s financials with storytelling

- Here are areas where financial information can be used to help support the story:
  - Begin with looking at the financial impact of the problem to the people affected by it
    - Cost of the problem
    - Cost of failed solutions
    - Size of the market
  - There is also the money spent in developing a workable solution
    - Research and development, prototyping, market testing, proof of concept
    - Sales to date, number of customers, testimonials, sales pipeline, number of stores selling your product
  - Current financial condition, projections, and what’s possible with funds raised
    - Sales forecasting, uses of funds, operating budget, cash flow projection, financial ratios, current and projected magic three reports
  - THE ASK
    - How much you need, structure of the offer (equity or debt), next step

## What  investors and banks want to know about your company

- Investors and banks have different criteria for evaluating companies to fund.
- For banks, funding companies it is not an investment. They are looking for a safe return on money loaned. What they want to see is:
  - Stability and consistent growth over time; at least 3 years of steady state financials
  - Additional benefits to the bank for helping the business grow, e.g., expanding banking business (new accounts, payroll processing, employee banking incentives, etc.)
  - Ability to service bank loan
  - Good character of founders and/or co-signers
- For investors, it is an investment. They are looking for potential for a significant return on their investment. What they want to see is:
  - Your vision and business are congruent with their expertise, experience, and their investment criteria
  - How much debt is on the books relative to equity
  - Marketing plans and sales projections
  - Ratios: financial, activity (key), solvency, liquidity, and profitability
  - Current financials: balance sheet, profit and loss statements, and cash flow statements
  - Current valuation

## Tailoring your presentation to their funding criteria

- Before making that first call, do your homework. Find out as much as possible about the company, bank, or investor to whom you are presenting
  - Who they are: name, location, CV, social media presence, LinkedIn profile
  - How they prefer to be solicited: in person, phone interview, video presentation, long or short meeting, multiple meeting and follow up, etc.
- What is their funding criteria: typical stage of company, industry or markets, funding size, founder profile, geography, specialty, business model, what’s possible, what’s probable
- Determine that you and the investor are a possible good fit
- Customize your pitch deck to reflect how you and the investor are a good match, and that you meet their criteria for funding

## How to stand in your company’s history, vision, and goals

- The act of taking a stand in and of itself does not need evidence. However, providing evidence to backup your stand, enables others to buy-in to your resolve
  - Your history demonstrates your passion, commitment, and leadership
  - Your vision is what you are building and what is possible for the business and the world
  - Your goals are clear and specific. Bold yet achievable
- Having done all this, you are well on your way to a “Yes”.
